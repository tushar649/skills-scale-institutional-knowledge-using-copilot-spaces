# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This directory provides a practical, lightweight project management framework for OctoAcme teams. The goal is to help teams start new work with clarity, execute in a consistent rhythm, communicate effectively, and improve continuously as work moves from idea to delivery. OctoAcme organizes work across five stages: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. Each stage complements the others and keeps teams aligned around scope, quality, ownership, and outcomes.

At initiation, projects validate the business need, success metrics, stakeholders, and rough timeline. The team captures this in a one-pager and confirms whether the work is ready to move into planning. Planning then translates the approved initiative into a backlog, milestones, risk register, and shared definition of done. During execution, teams use project boards, daily standups, pull requests, and quality gates to deliver work in small increments while tracking progress and escalating blockers when necessary.

The core roles in OctoAcme are designed to keep work accountable and collaborative. The Project Manager coordinates schedules, dependencies, and communication; the Product Lead defines the outcome and prioritization; developers build and test the solution; QA validates acceptance and quality; and stakeholders provide direction and approval. Communication is regular and structured: standups surface blockers, weekly syncs review progress and risks, demos keep stakeholders informed, and milestone updates maintain transparency. This rhythm helps teams make data-informed decisions and reduce avoidable surprises.

Quality assurance is a shared responsibility. OctoAcme expects unit and integration tests where relevant, CI checks for linting and validation, security scanning, smoke tests for critical flows, and manual QA when needed before release. Releases follow a defined checklist covering acceptance criteria, deployment readiness, rollback planning, testing, and post-release verification. After each sprint, milestone, or incident, the team conducts a retrospective to capture learnings and convert them into concrete action items that improve delivery over time.
